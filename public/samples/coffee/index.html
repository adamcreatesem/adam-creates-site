Coffee, poured at your table.

Roasted in front of you, ground by hand, and poured three rounds from the jebena. Popcorn on the side. Time taken. This is how coffee has been served in Ethiopia for a long time.

The Ceremony

Roasted at your table, poured three times.

The full ceremony takes about an hour. Beans are roasted in a pan in front of you, ground in a mortar, and brewed in the jebena. You get all three rounds, popcorn on the side, and the table stays yours for the time it takes.

1

Green Beans

Raw beans washed, then roasted in the pan at your table until they are dark and fragrant.

2

Ground by Hand

Ground in the mortar while hot, then brewed in the jebena over the coals.

3

Three Rounds

Abol, Tona, and Baraka -- the three rounds, each one different. You get all three.

Where the Beans Come From

Four regions, four cups.

We keep four single origins on the shelf and rotate by what is in. Ask what is in today -- the answer changes by the week.

Yirgacheffe

Light, floral, with a citrus edge. The one that tastes cleanest and brightest of the four.

Sidama

Berry and cocoa, a little heavier on the palate than Yirgacheffe. Familiar to most people who know Ethiopian coffee.

Harrar

Dark fruit and a winey note. The strongest of the four in flavor, the one people either love or do not.

Guji

Peach, honey, and a round body. The newest of the four on our shelf and the one most people ask for by name after the first time.
